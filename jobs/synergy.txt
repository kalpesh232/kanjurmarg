-> difference between merge, join and concatenate
### Corrected Explanation:

Concatenate (pd.concat()): Stacks DataFrames vertically (axis=0) or horizontally (axis=1). It does not merge based on common keys but aligns along the index. Missing values are filled with NaN.

Join (df.join()): Combines two DataFrames based on their index. It's a simpler way to merge on index rather than a specific column. By default, it performs a left join but can be adjusted.

Merge (pd.merge()): Combines DataFrames based on common columns or indices, similar to SQL joins (inner, left, right, outer). It's the most flexible but requires specifying the key(s).  In pd.merge(), the default join is inner.
